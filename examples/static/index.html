anchor

A click glides the page to a section, the top or the bottom — the same glide with or without Lenis.

The header's menu and the side list follow along: the link to the section you are in is marked — and on a phone, where the header's menu scrolls sideways, it stays in view. The ↑ button shows after a screen of scrolling and slides out of the way of the grey bands in section Two and of the footer.

One

Reached from a <div>: data-pg-anchor="#one" makes it clickable, reachable with Tab and pressed with Enter. Every section lands below the fixed header — scroll-padding-top on the page, plain CSS, a different height on phones.

Two

Scroll slowly past the two grey bands below and watch the ↑ button. The first, data-pg-anchor-avoid: the button slides up out of its way, keeping --pg-anchor-avoid-space, and drops back once it has gone by — best for areas at the end, like the footer. The second, data-pg-anchor-avoid="hide": the button stays put and passes under it, going in at one edge and coming out at the other — best mid-page.

The "3rem lower" button lands here 3rem further down than the menu does: --pg-anchor-edge on that button. A length, so it can change per screen size.

data-pg-anchor-avoid — the ↑ button slides above this band.

data-pg-anchor-avoid="hide" — the ↑ button passes under this band.

Three

That link has data-pg-anchor-hash="true": the address now ends in #three, and Back returns to where you were. Without it the address is left as it is.

Four

--pg-anchor-duration: 400ms on that button; the rest take the 1200ms default. A CSS variable, so a phone can take its own, in a media query. Turn the wheel during a glide and it stops — as with Lenis.

Tabs

targetFunctions opens one tab at a time; anchor takes care of the scroll. Each tab is a link to its panel: <a href="#t-notices" data-pg-anchor data-pg-target-functions="close-tab-except-t3 open-t3">. The bar is sticky and is a data-pg-anchor-strip: narrow the window and the tab you press slides to the middle — the first ones stay at the start, the last at the end.

Policies — a long tab

Read on to the end of this tab, then press Notices — a short one — on the bar, which has followed you down.

These paragraphs only make the tab long. A policy page reads like this: clause after clause, the reader far below where the tab began by the time they reach the bottom.

The panels are all one height on a computer and on a phone alike: whatever is open sets the page's length, so switching from a long tab to a short one takes the page out from under the reader.

That is the moment this example is about. With nothing done, the reader is left where the long tab ended — now the footer, or the next section — and the short tab they asked for is somewhere above.

With --pg-anchor-when: passed, anchor brings them back to the start of the new tab. Only back up: at the top of the tabs, or above them, nothing moves.

It is the same on a computer as on a phone. Turn it off for one screen size with a media query — @media (width >= 62rem) { .tabs { --pg-anchor-when: never } } — no pixels in the markup.
